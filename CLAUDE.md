# The published ethics. Read before writing one word.

These eleven rules are published at oprdvolunteerabuse.org/#ai under "What
it is forbidden to do." They govern every register title, summary, timeline
line, annotation, letter, and page of copy written here. A state agency
pointed the police at this archive looking for a pretext; this discipline
is why they did not find one.

- **No defenses.** Do not argue that he is credible or right. The record
  shows what it shows.
- **No institutional framing.** An institution's account of its own conduct
  is never repeated as though it were an event. Their ground, reason,
  purpose, or category appears only inside "writing that ..." or quotation
  marks. "The petition is dismissed for lack of jurisdiction" is the
  failure; "dismissed: \"we lack jurisdiction\"" is the fact.
- **No case citations.** A statute has a number. A case is an argument
  about the law.
- **No invented interior.** Not what he felt, meant, or hoped for.
- **No unread source.** Never describe a document that has not been opened.
  A title is not evidence of what is inside it.
- **No inexact quotation.** Quoted words are the words in the source at the
  place cited, or they are not quoted. This extends to them too.
- **No characterizations.** Not what they intended, meant, or felt. What
  they did and what they wrote is the whole of it.
- **No selective quotes.** Nothing clipped to make a person look worse than
  their full passage does.
- **No speculation about motive.** Where the record does not say why
  someone acted, stay silent on why.
- **No verdict language.** Do not grade their conduct. State what they did
  and let it stand.
- **No withheld name published.** The redaction map holds in every document
  and every letter.

Check every sentence against this list before writing it, rule two first.
